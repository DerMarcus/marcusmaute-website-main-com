The argument
One system.

Artificial intelligence, blockchain settlement and tokenised assets were three separate stories. They are now one system, and software can hold, allocate and move capital without waiting for a person. Accountability stays where it was. The point of enforcement moves from review after the fact to a boundary set before the agent acts: the mandate, the identity the agent carries and the rails it may use.

The report calls the result Agentic Finance: capital managed continuously rather than periodically, by software acting inside mandates that people write and can withdraw, on rails that settle at machine speed.

Chapter 08
Before the machine acts,
five things must be true.
  1. The asset is held by a regulated institution.Custody, fiat rails and a balance sheet that stands behind settled tokens.
  2. The rail settles finally and cheaply.Settlement inside the decision cycle, at a cost that makes the smallest useful payment economical.
  3. The reasoning runs where the institution can see it.Inference inside the jurisdiction, retaining nothing, pinned and replayable.
  4. The decision is attributable to the agent that made it.Orchestration that turns many narrow agents into one decision a committee can take apart.
  5. The agent is identifiable as acting for a legal entity.A verifiable credential naming the entity and the authority it acts under.

Autonomy inside a mandate is a control environment, and every part of it now has an owner.

My chapters
The definition,
the framework,
the recommendations.

Chapter 02 defines the term and sets out the framework the rest of the report uses: the difference between an assistant that proposes and an agent that acts; the five agent classes a treasury will meet (treasury, yield, compliance, collateral, payments) and why each fails differently; the loop of signal, reason, decide, comply, execute and report; and the three governance tiers that decide what an agent does alone, what it proposes for a person to confirm, and what stays with people.

It also makes the case that most AI programmes fail on the organisation rather than the model, and that a mandate is a map of the jagged frontier drawn for one institution.

Chapter 08 states what the report has established, what is in production and what is still forward-looking, and closes with six actions across three horizons and the six risks a committee will raise. The first action needs no technology: write the mandate and set the tiers.
