Where I plant my flag
Four positions.

The argument of the report, and of my work, in four sentences.

01
The mandate
The first step needs no technology
Before an institution buys a model, it writes the mandate: what the agent may do, within what limits, who confirms what, and where the line sits that it cannot cross. Every layer of the stack is bought; the mandate is the one thing no supplier can deliver.
02
The loop
The queues, not the steps, are where the capital goes
A human treasury team runs the same loop an agent does: signal, reason, decide, comply, execute, report. The difference is the queues between the steps. Remove them and capital that used to wait can move when it should.
03
AI transformation
The organisation, not the model
Buy the model and leave the process alone, and you get the pilot that impresses and the P&L that does not move. A mandate is a map of the jagged frontier drawn for one institution: autonomous where the agent is reliably competent, confirmed by a person near the edge, reserved to people beyond it.
04
The stack
The infrastructure is ready before the institutions are
Regulated custody, settlement in seconds, sovereign inference and multi-agent orchestration are in production today; verifiable agent identity is further along in standards than in deployment. What remains is organisational, and that is the part an institution controls.
What I believe

An assistant proposes. An agent acts. The moment the software that decides is also the software that acts, the question changes from which model to who wrote the mandate, and where is it enforced.

A boundary that exists only in a prompt is not a boundary. The limits have to live where the agent cannot reach them: in custody, in the token, in the identity it carries.

Most AI programmes fail on the organisation, not the model. The technical deployment takes weeks; the governance takes months. The first step needs no technology at all.

And the infrastructure is ready before the institutions are. That is the part an institution controls, and the part I work on.
